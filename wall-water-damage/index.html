Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Newport News Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
